# Happy Birthday Augustine (354–430 CE)

Augustine was a procrastinator and overthinker who stole pears, failed spectacularly at being good (at least by his own impossible standards), eventually became a saint, and spent the rest of his life writing about all of it. I actually read about him and resonated very well with him; I think a lot of overthinking, introspective people would too.

## Who Was Augustine? (The Setup)

So imagine you're Augustine—a smart, ambitious, well-educated guy in the Roman Empire:

<ul>
<li>Giving speeches for a living (a rhetoric professor—basically a debate and persuasion skills teacher)</li>
<li>Living with a woman you love (for 15 years!)</li>
<li>Fortunate in your career</li>
<li>Your mom is very religious and in continuous prayer mode—hoping you'll convert to Christianity</li>
</ul>

You're skeptical and wish to explore—saying something like, <em>"Mom, it's not a big deal, I'm into philosophy and exploration. I will get this sorted, just not now…"</em>

## The Problem (Internal War)

You feel empty inside. Something's missing—even when you think you have everything that the world considers a good, successful life. You know what you want to do or should do to be a good person, but you keep messing up—you can't stop doing things you later regret.

The comedic tension: he's incredibly self-aware but still keeps doing things he regrets.

## The Pear Incident (30 Years of Guilt)

When he was a teenager, he and his friends stole pears from a neighbor's tree. They didn't even eat the pears—they weren't good pears. They just threw them at pigs.

> Normal person: "Ah… I was a stupid kid, but it was fun."

> Meanwhile, Augustine: writes entire chapters… "Holy Christ! WHY did I do this? I wasn't hungry! I knew stealing was wrong, the fruit wasn't even good…"

Thirty years later, he's a bishop. Respected. Holy. And he STILL can't stop thinking about those pears.

## Feynman Translation

Augustine stole pears as a teen and couldn't forgive himself thirty years later. This tells us about his mind—he couldn't let go of anything.

## Imagine Augustine in Therapy

> **Therapist:** "So, what brings you in today?"  
> **Augustine:** "I stole pears when I was 16."  
>  
> **Therapist:** "Okay, you clearly don't steal pears now. Have you done anything regretful recently?"  
> **Augustine:** "Oh, I have a list. I'm literally writing all of it down. This all started when Adam and Eve ate that apple..."  
>  
> **Therapist's notes:** "There is a pattern of some issue with fruits."

## Life Journey

<ul>
<li>Lives with his girlfriend</li>
<li>They have a son together</li>
<li>He joins Manichaeism</li>
<li>Successful career, but he feels lost</li>
</ul>

## The Transformation (Garden Moment)

He's sitting in a garden, completely broken inside. He hears a child's voice: <em>"tolle lege"</em>—"Take up and read." He opens the Bible and reads a passage about giving up worldly pleasures.

**Dramatic conversion:** Something breaks open in his mind. He becomes a Christian on the spot.

## His Big Ideas

He was a very normal person. What made him a genius was the same thing that made him worried about everything—he pondered vague “WHY” questions, and sometimes came up with good answers.

### 1. Original Sin

**The problem:** Why do I keep doing bad stuff even when I know it's bad?

**His view:** Humans have bugs in their operating system (our minds)—and he blamed Adam and Eve.

### 2. Free Will vs God’s Plan

**The problem:** If God knows everything, do we have free will?  
**His attempt:** Humans have free will, but need God's grace to choose good.

### 3. Time

**The problem:** What is time?  
**Insight:** Time exists in the mind—memory (past), attention (present), expectation (future).

## The Transformation (Before and After)

**Before:** “I want to be good… but not YET. God, give me chastity and continence, but not now!”  
**After:** “Okay God, I'm all in. I'll write millions of words.”

## Where He Went Too Deep

Everything became about sin:

<ul>
<li>Babies? Sinful</li>
<li>Sex? Sinful</li>
<li>Thinking about sex? Sinful</li>
<li>Pear theft at 16? Lifetime guilt</li>
</ul>

**Feynman insight:** Humans have a conflict between desires and ideals; Augustine turned that into a lifelong analysis.

## Why This Matters Today

Even if we don’t agree with everything, Augustine explored the “why” behind human weakness.  
Internal conflicts are real, and reflecting on them can be a kind of therapy.
